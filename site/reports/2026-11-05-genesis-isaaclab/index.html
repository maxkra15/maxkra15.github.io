Measurement contract

Physics times synchronized stepping with prescribed controls; rollout includes actions, observations, rewards and resets. One environment transition contains the configured number of physics steps. Worker startup covers runtime imports through the first usable observation; Python/site initialization is outside this clock. The first step and warmed throughput are separate. Complete process wall time is also recorded. A fresh process can reuse disk caches.

The shared benchmark baseline uses Genesis's Newton solver with a 25-iteration maximum and native tolerance. Isaac Lab runs without Kit and uses Newton/MuJoCo Warp with Newton solving, implicitfast integration, 100 solver / 50 line-search iterations, tolerance 1e-6, pyramidal friction, MuJoCo contacts, one substep and CUDA graphs. These settings do not establish equal solver work or numerical accuracy. Stock ANYmal training presets use different substep/contact/friction settings; this shared task is a separate benchmark baseline.

ANYmal physics runs can fall or settle under prescribed controls; throughput alone does not establish walking quality. The backend metadata records controls and assets. Genesis uses Madrona rasterization and Isaac Lab uses Newton Warp ray tracing. RGB augments proprioception in the common CNN policy; these tasks do not establish vision-only navigation. Renderer quality differs, and image throughput does not establish quality equivalence. MJWarp graph activity and fatal capacity flags are checked at the final solver state. Convergence-limit flags are retained separately and do not reject a run. Final flags are not a cumulative history.

Comparisons share task, vision mode, mode, batch size, timestep, decimation, image dimensions and measured step count. Maximum capacity is separate from speed at a matched batch size. Successful-run summaries retain their sample count and observed range; they are not confidence intervals. Hardware and shared training dependencies must match. Engine-specific simulation and rendering packages differ and are pinned below.

Learning curves use each seed’s held-out survival and task error against wall time and transitions. The target requires survival ≥0.95 and error ≤0.25 m/s for ANYmal, ≤0.1 rad for cartpole, or ≤0.05 m for Franka. Evaluation uses automatic resets after failure: survival means no failure at any point in the evaluation horizon; for Franka this means finite joint state within joint limits plus a 0.1 rad margin, rather than no fall. Its evaluation lasts 5 s. Reported reward and error also include steps after resets. Training time includes periodic evaluation but excludes model/optimizer creation, simulator startup and checkpoint saving. Complete process time includes those costs and teardown. Asset equivalence and useful final behavior are necessary before claiming a learning advantage.

Why repeat this comparison?

GPUSimBench reported approximately 6× Franka throughput for Genesis using different maximum batch sizes (131,072 versus 32,768), with rendering and learning excluded. Its public implementation also compares Genesis scene stepping with an Isaac Lab task step. These results motivate matched workloads; they do not establish a general training speedup. That study used an older Isaac Lab/PhysX stack. This MJWarp comparison changes the backend and versions, so it is not a direct RTX 5070 reproduction.
